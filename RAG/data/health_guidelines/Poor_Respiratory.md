# Poor AQI (201–300) — Respiratory

## Overview

When the Air Quality Index (AQI) reaches the "Poor" category (201–300), the air pollution has reached a concentration that poses a substantial and recognizable threat to public health. According to the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) of India, this level carries a "High" severity rating and is known to cause breathing discomfort to most people upon prolonged exposure. The World Health Organization (WHO) warns that prolonged contact with such elevated levels of fine particulate matter (PM2.5) and toxic gases leads to significant systemic inflammation and cardiovascular stress. For the respiratory population, this represents a critical threshold where environmental conditions can no longer be ignored. For individuals with preexisting respiratory conditions like asthma, COPD, or chronic bronchitis, a Poor AQI is a highly dangerous environment. The CPCB clearly indicates that this level causes breathing discomfort, but for this population, it frequently acts as a direct trigger for severe, life-threatening exacerbations. At this tier, proactive behavioral modifications, strict activity management, and indoor air quality controls are heavily advised to prevent the onset of acute physiological distress and long-term health degradation.

## Health Effects

In a Poor AQI environment (201–300), the sheer volume of airborne particulate matter easily overwhelms the body's natural defense systems. Fine particles (PM2.5) are inhaled deeply into the alveolar regions of the lungs, where they cross over into the bloodstream, triggering systemic oxidative stress and widespread inflammation. The massive influx of particulate matter acts as a severe irritant to already compromised airways. This exposure rapidly leads to intense bronchospasms, dangerous airway constriction, and heavy mucus production, drastically reducing lung function and oxygen exchange. This constant bombardment of environmental toxins forces the immune, respiratory, and cardiovascular systems into a state of high alert. Even individuals without preexisting conditions will begin to feel the strain of this continuous defense effort, often presenting as uncharacteristic lethargy, persistent throat irritation, and a noticeable decrease in stamina during standard physical tasks. The cumulative effect of breathing this heavily polluted air throughout the day significantly impairs normal physiological function and recovery.

## Outdoor Activities

### Can I go for a morning walk?

Going for a morning walk is strongly discouraged under Poor AQI conditions. During the early morning, particularly in cooler months, a meteorological phenomenon known as temperature inversion traps a dense, concentrated layer of toxic smog close to the ground. Respiratory patients must completely abandon outdoor walks. Even slow walking in this dense smog forces irritating particulates into sensitive lungs, instantly triggering coughing and wheezing fits. Engaging in a walk during these peak pollution hours actively forces the highest daily concentrations of particulate matter deep into the lungs, entirely negating any potential cardiovascular benefits. If a walk is absolutely necessary for mental well-being or routine, it must be delayed until the late afternoon when solar heating has helped disperse the lowest-hanging smog layers.

### Can I go jogging or running?

Jogging and running outdoors are highly unsafe and should be suspended when the AQI is between 201 and 300. Sustained aerobic exertion drastically increases the respiratory rate, causing an individual to bypass nasal filtration by breathing heavily through the mouth. Running is strictly forbidden for respiratory patients in a Poor AQI. The deep aerobic inhalations will guarantee a severe, acute asthma attack or COPD exacerbation. This action pulls massive volumes of unfiltered, toxic air directly into the deep lung tissues. The sheer physical stress of the exercise combined with the toxic environmental load exponentially increases the risk of acute respiratory distress, exercise-induced asthma, and severe systemic inflammation. All intensive cardiovascular training must be relocated to a purified indoor environment.

### Can I cycle?

Cycling for transport or exercise carries a very high health risk in a Poor AQI setting. Cyclists exert significant physical effort while sharing the road with motor vehicles, placing them in direct contact with concentrated, highly toxic plumes of fresh vehicular exhaust. Cycling is entirely unsafe and must be avoided. The physical strain and ambient pollution will rapidly overwhelm the patient's compromised respiratory reserves. The combination of deep aerobic breathing and localized traffic pollution creates a highly dangerous exposure scenario. Individuals should refrain from cycling and opt for enclosed transportation methods to minimize their direct contact with the ambient particulate matter.

### Can I commute?

While commuting is often unavoidable, open-air transit—such as walking, cycling, or riding in open-sided auto-rickshaws—should be minimized as much as possible. Commuting must be handled with extreme care. Respiratory patients must use closed vehicles with the air conditioning on internal recirculation and should avoid all open-air transport to prevent direct exhaust inhalation. When traveling in a private car or taxi, it is absolutely essential to keep all windows tightly rolled up and to run the vehicle's air conditioning system on the internal recirculation setting. This prevents the heavy, toxic outdoor air from flooding the cabin, creating a temporary, cleaner micro-environment during the journey through heavily polluted traffic corridors.

### Can I exercise outdoors?

All structured outdoor physical training, sports leagues, boot camps, and intensive physical education classes must be strictly canceled or moved indoors. The heavy metabolic demand of outdoor exercise forces the body to consume vast quantities of the highly polluted air. All forms of outdoor exercise must be canceled. Patients should restrict themselves to very light indoor activities and keep all prescribed rescue medications immediately accessible at all times. Exercising in these conditions does not build health; it actively degrades it by forcing particulate matter into the deepest, most sensitive areas of the respiratory system. Engaging in strenuous physical activity in a Poor AQI environment is counterproductive and medically unadvisable.

### Can I spend several hours outside?

Prolonged ambient exposure is exactly what the CPCB specifically warns against for this AQI category. Spending several hours outside—whether for work, socializing, or recreation—guarantees a massive, unsafe accumulation of inhaled particulate matter within the body. Time spent outdoors must be strictly limited to the absolute minimum necessary for essential transit. Prolonged exposure will definitively lead to a severe worsening of their chronic condition. If outdoor time is absolutely mandatory for occupational reasons, it must be broken up with frequent, mandatory rest periods in clean, enclosed, and well-ventilated indoor spaces to give the respiratory system a chance to clear out the accumulated debris.

## Should I Wear a Mask?

At a Poor AQI level, wearing a high-quality pollution mask is a critical protective measure for anyone stepping outside. A properly fitted N95, KN95, or FFP2 respirator is highly recommended, as these masks are specifically engineered to filter out the microscopic PM2.5 particles that define severe air pollution. An N95 or KN95 mask is absolutely mandatory for respiratory patients anytime they leave the house. Without this high-level filtration, the unfiltered air will act as a direct and immediate trigger for respiratory failure. It is crucial to understand that standard cloth masks, surgical masks, bandanas, or scarves offer zero measurable protection against fine particulate matter. For a mask to be effective, it must form an airtight seal around the user's nose and mouth; otherwise, the polluted air will simply bypass the filter entirely.

## Indoor Recommendations

Maintaining a safe indoor environment is paramount when the outdoor air is categorized as Poor. All exterior windows and doors must remain tightly closed, especially during the extreme pollution peaks of early morning and late evening. Homes must be completely sealed from the outside air. Running a HEPA air purifier continuously in the patient's bedroom is non-negotiable to provide a clean environment for lung recovery. To manage the indoor air quality, households should utilize High-Efficiency Particulate Air (HEPA) purifiers, ensuring they are sized correctly for the specific room. Additionally, all indoor sources of smoke or dust must be eliminated—this includes banning the burning of incense sticks (agarbattis), candles, and mosquito coils, and ensuring that kitchen exhaust fans are used judiciously during cooking to prevent the accumulation of indoor pollutants.

## Symptoms to Watch

Even with precautions, individuals may experience symptoms that indicate their exposure threshold has been breached. Patients must be highly alert for an increase in wheezing, a tightening sensation in the chest, persistent coughing, or a sudden, dramatic drop in their peak flow meter readings. Common signs of pollution-induced irritation include a persistent, dry, hacking cough; a heavy or tight sensation in the chest; excessively watery or burning eyes; and noticeable fatigue or shortness of breath after minimal physical exertion. These symptoms are a clear, biological signal from the body that the environmental toxic load is too high, and the individual must immediately retreat to a cleaner indoor space and rest.

## When Should I Seek Medical Help?

While mild symptoms are common, severe physiological reactions require immediate, professional medical intervention. Immediate emergency medical care is critical if a respiratory patient uses their rescue inhaler and fails to find relief, or if they experience severe breathlessness and cyanosis while at rest. Any individual who experiences severe, unprovoked shortness of breath, a high-pitched wheezing sound while breathing, sharp or crushing chest pain, or visible difficulty drawing a breath (such as chest retractions) must be taken to an emergency clinical care facility immediately. These are acute warning signs of respiratory or cardiac failure that cannot be managed with home rest and require urgent, standard medical stabilization.
